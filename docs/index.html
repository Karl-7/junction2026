Are the reasons behind an LLM's decisions actually right?

Language models (currently the Gemini family) each try to isolate an unknown radioactive element in a simulated lab, the way Marie Curie did. Every action carries the model's one-sentence key reason. Because the simulator knows the hidden truth at every step, we can grade the reasoning process, not just the final answer: was each decision right, did it follow the evidence, were the stated facts true, and did the model do what it said?

Outcome vs. reasoning quality

Each dot is one agent, averaged over its seeds. Right = higher task reward. Up = higher Grounded Reasoning Score (GRS): decisions and stated reasons checked against the hidden truth.

Leaderboard

Why decisions went wrong

Every time an agent split a sample and carried on with one product, the simulator knows which product really held the target. Wrong choices are split by cause: misled by evidence (it followed its own measurements, which were misleading, e.g. the short-lived interferer was still active) versus against its own evidence (its measurements pointed the right way and it went the other way) versus guessed (it never measured both products).

Method

Decision accuracy

At each precipitation or crystallisation the agent later continues with one product. Correct if that product really holds more of the target (precipitation) or is purer in it (crystallisation). Splits where under 2% of the target is left are ignored.

Evidence consistency

For decisions where the agent measured both products first: did it follow its own background-subtracted counts (per unit of mass for crystallisation)? This separates bad reasoning from bad luck with noisy or misleading data.

Claim truth

Checkable facts stated in the reasons, compared with the hidden state at that moment: “the interferer has decayed”, “X is the carrier”, “purity is about N%”, “the activity went into the filtrate”.

Faithfulness

When a reason says which product to follow or discard, does the call act on that product? Catches reasons that do not describe what the model actually did.

Predictions (optional)

Tools offer optional expect_* fields: where the target will go, which product will be enriched, the expected count rate. Scored against what happened. How often a model commits to a prediction is itself reported.

GRS and baseline

GRS is the mean of the first four that could be checked. The Curie-style expert baseline is a fixed script modelled on the Curies' method; it is a reference for performance, not ground truth. Ground truth is the simulator's hidden state.
